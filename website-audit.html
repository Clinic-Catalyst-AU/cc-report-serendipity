Quick wins

Five fixes with the biggest payoff

Win 1

Put Canning Vale in your page titles

Your page titles are the blue headline people click in Google. Right now most read like "Massage - Serendipity Medi Spa". Adding the treatment and the suburb tells Google, and the person searching, that you are the local answer.

Win 2

Write the two lines Google shows under your title

Every page is missing its description, so Google grabs whatever text it finds, often the sign-up popup. A warm, specific description gets more clicks. Here are three to start with:

Home: "Facials, skin treatments, massage and spa packages at Ranford Shopping Centre, Canning Vale. A warm, experienced team who love mature skin. Book online."

Massage: "Relaxation and tension-relieving massage in Canning Vale, plus spa packages for two hours of pure switch-off. Book online in a couple of taps."

Spa packages: "Spa packages and gift vouchers in Canning Vale, from a 90-minute melt to our two-hour Pure Delight with massage and facial. Buy online in minutes."

Win 3

Give every page one clear main heading

49 pages have no main heading, including your homepage, massage page and skin needling page, and 11 pages have several competing ones. One clear heading per page, with the treatment and the suburb in it, is one of the simplest signals you can send Google. For the homepage: Facials, Skin Treatments and Day Spa in Canning Vale.

Win 4

Describe your images

Google cannot see photos, it reads the short description behind them. 541 images have none. Describing them in plain words (for example "Two-hour Pure Delight spa package at Serendipity Medi Spa, Canning Vale") helps you show up in image search and helps anyone using a screen reader.

Win 5

Tell Google your business details directly

There is a behind-the-scenes format Google reads to understand a local business: your name, address, phone, opening hours and services. None of your pages have it. Adding it to the homepage and contact page helps you appear in Google Maps and the local results for "day spa near me". It is a one-off job.

Bigger opportunities

Pages that could bring you new clients

Massage

A proper massage and day spa page

Grace, you said it on our call: everyone is Googling "massage spa Canning Vale" and there is not much nearby. Your massage page sits at /massage-2/ with no main heading. Rebuilding it as the go-to "Massage and Day Spa in Canning Vale" page, with your packages, what to expect and a few common questions answered, is the single biggest search opportunity we found.

Mature skin

A mature skin page

Mature skin is your focus, yet only 3 pages mention it. One page that speaks to the concern first (dullness, fine lines, loss of firmness), then walks through the treatments you would recommend and why, gives Google and your future clients one clear place to land.

Gift vouchers

A Pure Delight gift page for Christmas

Vouchers are already how many new clients find you. A dedicated Pure Delight gift page, set up so we can see every voucher sale come through, is the foundation for a Christmas campaign. With Christmas close, this is the one to do first.

Questions

Answer the questions your clients already ask

Your blog has two posts. Short, honest answers to the questions your team hears every day ("How many IPL sessions will I need?", "Is skin needling right for mature skin?") are what Google quotes at the top of results. These double as reels and stories, so one answer does three jobs.

Skin assessment

A simple skin assessment on the site

A few questions about someone's skin, their concern and what they have tried, ending in a suggested treatment and a booking. It turns a browser into an enquiry and tells your team exactly who is coming in.

Keeping you protected

A few words to soften

Australian advertising rules are strict about promises in health and beauty. A few phrases on the site are worth softening so you are never caught out:
